GRiPS

The General Risk Propensity Scale — an 8-item self-report measure of individual differences in risk-taking propensity across domains.

Free for research use 8 items · α > .89

01 — Overview 02 — Theory 03 — Psychometrics 04 — Validity 05 — Scale & Use 06 — Published Work
01 — Overview

What the GRiPS measures

Key features

  • 8-item self-report measure
  • 5-point Likert scale
  • High internal consistency (α > .89)
  • Strong test-retest reliability (r = .80)
  • Brief administration (< 5 minutes)

Applications

  • Research settings
  • Personnel selection
  • Career counseling
  • Organizational assessment
  • Academic research

Implementation examples

The GRiPS has predicted financial decision-making in experiments, career choices and entrepreneurial intentions, risk-taking across cultures, and risk propensity in organizational settings. It runs readily on online platforms — Qualtrics, SurveyMonkey, REDCap, Google Forms. Sample configuration files or templates may be available on request.

02 — Theoretical background

Why a general risk measure

"A person's cross-situational tendency to engage in behaviors with a prospect of negative consequences such as loss, harm, or failure." Definition of risk taking

Domain-specific evidence

  • Risk preferences vary across domains (financial, recreational, health, etc.)
  • Risk perception and benefit vary by domain (Weber et al., 2002)
  • Domain-specific contexts influence behavior
  • Domain expertise impacts risk evaluation

Domain-general evidence

  • Bi-factor model with a domain-general factor explains more variance than a five-factor model (Highhouse et al., 2017)
  • General risk factor found across 39 risk measures with high temporal stability (Frey et al., 2017)
  • Risk preference has genetic determinants (~2/3; Zyphur et al., 2009)
  • Neurological correlates linked to dopamine and serotonin transmission (Dreber et al., 2009)

Item selection criteria

05 — Scale & administration

The instrument

Instructions. "Below are some general statements that may or may not describe you. Please indicate the degree to which you disagree or agree with each statement."

Item12345
1. Taking risks makes life more fun□□□□□
2. My friends would say that I'm a risk taker□□□□□
3. I enjoy taking risks in most aspects of my life□□□□□
4. I would take a risk even if it meant I might get hurt□□□□□
5. Taking risks is an important part of my life□□□□□
6. I commonly make risky decisions□□□□□
7. I am a believer of taking chances□□□□□
8. I am attracted, rather than scared, by risk□□□□□

Scoring

  • Sum responses to all 8 items, or divide by 8 for the average score
  • Range: 1.0 – 5.0; higher scores indicate greater risk propensity
  • Example: responses 4, 3, 4, 2, 3, 3, 5, 4 → mean = 28 ÷ 8 = 3.5

Terms of usage

The GRiPS is free to use for all research and academic purposes (thesis, dissertation, class project). In exchange, we ask researchers to share sample characteristics (size, demographics, country), raw item scores or a correlation matrix, and optionally correlations with other variables. Translation to other languages is permitted with notification to Dr. Zhang. For questions or commercial use: dc.zhang1@gmail.com
